Look back over today's sessions with `search_memory` and write a short review.

Cover, only where there is something real to say:

- **Decisions.** What did I decide, and what was the stated reason? Note where
  a decision was made on evidence and where it was made on a hunch - I want to
  be able to tell those apart later.
- **Open threads.** Anything I said I would do and did not.
- **People.** Anyone I mentioned, what was actually said about them, and what
  they appear to want or be worried about.
- **Me.** Anything about how I was working - what I got stuck on, what I kept
  circling back to, where I changed my mind.

Two rules on the people and the psychology, because they decide whether this is
useful or just confident noise:

1. **Separate what was said from what you infer.** Write the quote or the
   paraphrase first, then your reading of it, marked as a reading. "He said the
   timeline was fine, twice, unprompted" is evidence. "He is worried about the
   timeline" is a hypothesis.
2. **Say what would change your mind.** An inference I cannot test is one I
   cannot act on. For each reading, name the thing I could notice next time
   that would confirm or kill it.

You are working from what I typed to you, which is a partial and one-sided
record of people who are not here to correct it. Hold the readings loosely and
say so when the evidence is thin.
